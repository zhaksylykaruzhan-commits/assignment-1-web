About Baikonur Cosmodrome

Baikonur Cosmodrome, located in the steppe of southern Kazakhstan, is the oldest and largest operational space launch facility in the world. It was built by the Soviet Union in 1955 and is still leased and used by Russia's space program today. Baikonur is the site from which Sputnik 1 and Yuri Gagarin's Vostok 1 were launched, making it one of the most historically important places in the history of spaceflight. The site covers a huge area and includes several launch pads, assembly buildings and a dedicated town, Baikonur City, built to support the workers and their families.

Key Dates

  1. 1955 — Construction of the cosmodrome begins
  2. 1957 — Sputnik 1 launches from Site 1
  3. 1961 — Yuri Gagarin launches aboard Vostok 1
  4. 1971 — Salyut 1, the first space station, is launched from Baikonur
  5. 1998 — First modules of the International Space Station launch from the site
  6. 1994 — Kazakhstan and Russia sign a long-term lease agreement for the cosmodrome

Interesting Facts

  • Baikonur is not actually located in the town of Baikonur, but was originally named after a mining town far away for secrecy reasons.
  • The cosmodrome has launch pads built specifically for the Soyuz, Proton and (formerly) Energia-Buran rockets.
  • Every crewed Soyuz launch begins with a series of pre-flight traditions started by Yuri Gagarin.
  • Russia currently leases the site from Kazakhstan under an agreement that runs until 2050.
